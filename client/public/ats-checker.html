How it works

Four dimensions.
One honest score.

ResumeIQ uses GPT-4o to score your resume on the same criteria ATS systems and recruiters use to filter candidates.

01
ATS Format
Single column only. No tables, text boxes, or columns. No images or icons. Contact info in the body, not header/footer. Standard section headings. Plain DOCX output.
02
Bullet Quality
Every bullet needs a strong action verb and a metric. "Grew revenue 40%" beats "Responsible for revenue." No passive language, no vague claims without evidence.
03
Keyword Alignment
Does your resume use the language recruiters search for? Industry terms, tool names, role-specific vocabulary. ATS is literal — it looks for exact matches, not synonyms.
04
Completeness
Contact info, LinkedIn URL, consistent dates, professional summary, education. Missing any of these flags the resume as incomplete — and some ATS auto-reject on missing fields.

An ATS resume checker tests your resume against Applicant Tracking System requirements — the software companies use to filter resumes before a human reads them. How do I know if my resume is ATS compatible?
Upload your resume to ResumeIQ. We score it 1–10 on ATS Format, Keyword Alignment, Bullet Quality, and Completeness. A score under 7 on any dimension means ATS is likely filtering you out. Scores under 5 mean you're being rejected automatically before any human review.
What makes a resume fail ATS?
The most common ATS failures are: two-column layouts (columns confuse parsers and drop half your content), tables and text boxes (invisible to most ATS), images or icons in the document, contact information stored in headers or footers, missing keywords from the job description, and creative PDF templates. A plain single-column DOCX almost always performs better than a designed PDF.
