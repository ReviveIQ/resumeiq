ATS & Resume Optimization
ATS stands for Applicant Tracking System — software used by employers to automatically screen resumes before a human reads them. According to the 2024 Ebsta × Pavilion B2B Sales Benchmark, 79% of resumes never make it past ATS filters — not because the candidate is unqualified, but because of formatting issues, missing keywords, or bullets that describe job duties instead of measurable outcomes.
An ATS-friendly resume uses:

• Single-column layout — no tables, text boxes, columns, or graphics
• Standard section headings — Summary, Experience, Education, Skills
• Action verb bullets with measurable outcomes where possible
• Keywords that match the job description exactly — not synonyms
• Contact info in the body — not headers or footers
• Plain .docx format — not a designed PDF

ResumeIQ outputs resumes in this exact format.
ResumeIQ scores resumes on 4 dimensions: Format (single column, no tables, correct headings), Bullet Quality (action verbs, measurable outcomes), Keywords (industry terminology, job description alignment), and Completeness (all critical sections present). Each dimension is scored 1–10. ResumeIQ shows your score before and after transformation so you can see the exact improvement.
A single-column reverse-chronological Word document (.docx) with standard headings, clean fonts, bullets starting with action verbs, and contact info in the body — not a header. No tables, columns, text boxes, icons, or graphics. This is exactly what ResumeIQ outputs.
You don't need revenue figures to write strong bullets. Every job has proxy metrics: tickets resolved per week, response time reduced, error rate decreased, onboarding cycle shortened, team size, accounts managed, customer satisfaction score. If no number exists, strong bullets describe scope and outcome without fabricating figures — "Managed enterprise accounts across a 4-state territory serving C-suite stakeholders" is specific and credible without a dollar amount. ResumeIQ never invents metrics.
Use exact keyword matching — ATS systems scan for literal matches, not synonyms. If the JD says "customer success", use "customer success" — not "client management". If it says "cross-functional alignment", use those exact words. ResumeIQ aligns your resume to recruiter keyword patterns automatically during transformation.
Under 10 years of experience: one page. Senior professionals and executives with 10+ years of relevant experience: two pages. ATS systems do not penalize for length. Every line should earn its place — a thin second page should be cut.

Working With Me
A "Working With Me" section is a professional behavioral summary that tells hiring managers how you work — your communication style, decision-making approach, how you perform under pressure, and what brings out your best work. ResumeIQ synthesizes it from personality assessment results (DISC, MBTI, Predictive Index, TKI). It answers the question every hiring manager has but cannot assess from a work history alone. No other resume tool offers this section.
DISC, MBTI, Predictive Index, and TKI describe how a person thinks, communicates, and behaves at work. ResumeIQ synthesizes results from one or more assessments into a professional "Working With Me" section written in behavioral language — not assessment jargon. This gives hiring managers a data point for culture fit that would otherwise require multiple interviews to surface.
Hiring managers spend 6–8 seconds on the first pass. They look at the first bullet under each role, the companies, the titles, and the dates — in that order. The first bullet either stops the scroll or it doesn't. A strong first bullet has a number, a scope, and an outcome. The Working With Me section gives them the cultural signal most resumes never provide.
